        The wall

        This is how a normal to-do list can feel.

        For an ADHD, dyslexic or anxious brain, “one task” is rarely one task — and a wall of items stops being a plan and becomes a verdict.

        Illustration: an overcrowded to-do list with 26 items, tiny controls, overdue badges and no sense of order.

        • Clean the kitchen overdue 3d
        • Email Prof. Reyes about thesis draft
        • Laundry overdue 5d
        • Buy birthday gift for Mom !!
        • Read 40 pages & take notes
        • Call dentist — reschedule
        • Finish scholarship form due tomorrow
        • Meal prep???
        • Fix bike tire
        • Reply to 12 emails
        • Term paper outline overdue 1w
        • Buy groceries (list?? where)
        • Water the plants
        • Cancel subscription
        • Return library books fine +$6
        • Practice presentation
        • Backup laptop
        • Organize photos
        • Study for stats midterm in 3 days
        • Print handout for meeting
        • Fix resume
        • Register for next term opens today
        • Call grandma
        • Deep clean the kitchen
        • Sort the laundry into the right piles and then…
        • Figure out life??? overdue always

        Stepwise refuses to show you this wall.

        It breaks the wall into pebbles, hands you exactly one pebble at a time, and keeps the rest politely out of sight.
